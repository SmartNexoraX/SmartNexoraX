About

This project turns your phone or computer into a remote control for a homemade RC car. The car runs an ESP32 board, and the controller talks to it over Bluetooth Low Energy straight from the browser.

The controller can also help people with limited mobility. From a phone, over Bluetooth, someone who finds it hard to move around or would rather not ask for help can drive the car themselves and, in a broader build, have it fetch everyday items for them.

The same setup can carry first-aid kits, food and water into places people cannot easily or safely enter, such as debris, tight spaces and hazardous areas. The operator drives from a safe distance and keeps watch on the car from their phone.

It was built for easy setup: one web page, big touch controls, and safety built in. The car stops by itself if the connection drops or the app loses focus.

What you get

Touch sliders and live dashboard

Steer with the vertical slider on the left and set throttle on the horizontal slider along the bottom, with a power gauge, turbo, cruise and a big emergency-stop button.

PC controls

Drive with W, A, S, D or the arrow keys, or drag the on-screen sliders with your mouse.

Gamepad support

Plug in an Xbox or PlayStation style controller and drive with the triggers or the left stick.

Headlights, horn, battery

Switch the lights, horn and hazard blinkers and see the battery voltage live.

Built-in safety

The car stops when you let go, press E-STOP, close the page or lose Bluetooth. The firmware also stops it after half a second without commands.

ESP32 firmware and guides

Ready-to-flash Arduino code with wiring, testing and troubleshooting guides for L298N or TB6612FNG motor drivers.

How to start

  1. Flash the ESP32 and wire the motor driver.
  2. Open the controller in Chrome (Android) or Chrome/Edge (PC).
  3. Tap Connect and choose RC-CAR-ESP32.
  4. Drive. Lift the wheels off the ground for the first test.
